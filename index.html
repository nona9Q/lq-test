How Lucky Are You?

This test measures your Luck Quotient (LQ) using a series of random trials. Your result is compared with the theoretical average and shown on a scale with a mean of 100 and a standard deviation of 15.

Number of trials: 60
Average LQ: 100
Standard deviation: 15
Time limit: 5 minutes

The Test

The test contains five different luck examinations. You must make each decision before the test clock reaches zero.

About LQ

LQ means Luck Quotient. It is a score which shows how far a set of random results moved above or below the mathematical average.

LQ 100 is the theoretical average. The display scale uses a standard deviation of 15.

The probabilities used by this test are known in advance, so the average and standard deviation can be calculated without collecting test results from other visitors.

Test Guide

The LQ Test contains 60 trials in five sections.

  1. Two Choice - select A or B.
  2. Four Cards - select one of four cards.
  3. Dice - guess the next die result.
  4. Trap Avoidance - avoid one trap among five positions.
  5. Rare Event - test for a 10 percent GOLD event.

The time limit is five minutes. The clock begins with the first actual test section and continues between sections.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Why is the average 100?
A. The theoretical mean is converted to 100 for the LQ scale.

Q. Why is the standard deviation 15?
A. LQ uses 15 as its display scale.

Q. Will I get the same result again?
A. Probably not. Each test generates new random outcomes.

Q. Does waiting before clicking improve my chances?
A. No. The probability of each trial does not change.

Score Calculation

Each trial is standardized using its known success probability:

s = (x - p) / sqrt(p(1 - p))

The combined score is converted to a z-score and then to the LQ scale:

LQ = 100 + 15z
